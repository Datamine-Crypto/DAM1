# Doing: a past verb between two names is the relation that joins them, as wrote and saw are, so
# who did it is asked with the same verb. Every verb has two learned lines, since a word in one
# line only reads with the default row and joins the name before it. Each scene tells three or four
# deeds, then asks who did each, the one a regular verb was done to, and a deed not told.
tom met sally. ann helped ben. max called lily. sue visited sam. => tom met sally; ann helped ben; max called lily; sue visited sam | who met sally? = tom; who helped ben? = ann; who called lily? = max; who visited sam? = sue; who did ann help? = ben; who did max call? = lily; who did sue visit? = sam; who met ben? = nothing; who called sam? = nothing
max met the doctor. sue helped the teacher. ben called the farmer. lily visited the king. => max met doctor; sue helped teacher; ben called farmer; lily visited king | who met the doctor? = max; who helped the teacher? = sue; who called the farmer? = ben; who visited the king? = lily; who did sue help? = teacher; who did ben call? = farmer; who did lily visit? = king; who helped the doctor? = nothing; who met the king? = nothing
ben found a key. tom found a ring. lily liked the song. sam liked the cake. => ben found key; tom found ring; lily liked song; sam liked cake | who found the key? = ben; who found the ring? = tom; who liked the song? = lily; who liked the cake? = sam; who found a key? = ben; what did lily like? = song; what did sam like? = cake; who liked the ring? = nothing; who found the cake? = nothing
