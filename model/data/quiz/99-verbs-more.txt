# More doing: each past verb between two names is the relation that joins them, so who did it is
# asked with the same verb. Two learned lines for every verb, since a word in one line only reads
# with the default row and joins the name before it. Each scene tells three or four deeds, then asks
# who did each, the one each was done to, and a deed not told.
kim pushed joe. eve painted the fence. ann cooked the soup. sue watched the game. => kim pushed joe; eve painted fence; ann cooked soup; sue watched game | who pushed joe? = kim; who painted the fence? = eve; who cooked the soup? = ann; who watched the game? = sue; who did kim push? = joe; what did eve paint? = fence; what did ann cook? = soup; what did sue watch? = game; who pushed the cart? = nothing
ray pushed the cart. joe painted the boat. max cooked the fish. ben watched lily. => ray pushed cart; joe painted boat; max cooked fish; ben watched lily | who pushed the cart? = ray; who painted the boat? = joe; who cooked the fish? = max; who watched lily? = ben; what did ray push? = cart; what did joe paint? = boat; what did max cook? = fish; who did ben watch? = lily; who cooked the soup? = nothing
tom thanked ann. lily thanked the nurse. sam followed max. kim followed the dog. => tom thanked ann; lily thanked nurse; sam followed max; kim followed dog | who thanked ann? = tom; who thanked the nurse? = lily; who followed max? = sam; who followed the dog? = kim; who did tom thank? = ann; who did lily thank? = nurse; who did sam follow? = max; what did kim follow? = dog; who thanked max? = nothing
