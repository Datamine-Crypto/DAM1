# Totals: two people each with a count of the same thing have the two counts added together, asked with
# together, in total or altogether. The traced lines and the turns stay as single lessons; each scene
# tells two people with counts of two things, so they means the two, then asks totals in every
# wording, each count, who has more and how many more.
turn: tom has 3 apples. ann has 2 apples. how many apples do they have together? {eom} => | 5
turn: sam has 4 pens. lily has 6 pens. how many pens do they have in total? {eom} => | 10
turn: ben has 5 cards. zoe has 5 cards. how many cards do they have altogether? {eom} => | 10
turn: kim has 7 toys. joe has 1 toy. how many toys do they have together? {eom} => | 8
tom has 3 apples. ann has 2 apples. tom has 4 pens. ann has 6 pens. => 3 = 3; tom owns apples; apples is 3; 2 = 2; ann owns apples; apples is 2; 4 = 4; tom owns pens; pens is 4; 6 = 6; ann owns pens; pens is 6 | how many apples do they have together? = 5; how many pens do they have in total? = 10; how many pens do they have altogether? = 10; how many apples do tom and ann have? = 5; how many apples does ann have? = 2; how many pens does tom have? = 4; who has more apples? = tom; who has more pens? = ann; how many more pens does ann have than tom? = 2
kim has 5 cards. joe has 5 cards. kim has 7 toys. joe has 1 toy. => 5 = 5; kim owns cards; cards is 5; joe owns cards; 7 = 7; kim owns toys; toys is 7; 1 = 1; joe owns toy; toy is 1 | how many cards do they have altogether? = 10; how many toys do they have together? = 8; how many cards do they have in total? = 10; how many cards do kim and joe have? = 10; how many cards does joe have? = 5; how many toys does kim have? = 7; who has more toys? = kim; how many more toys does kim have than joe? = 6
